## EN
Azobe, also known commercially as "Ekki," belongs to the Ochnaceae family and grows in West and Central Africa. Its wood ranges from dark red to chocolate-brown or purplish-brown, with prominent white deposits within the wood pores, a coarse texture, and interlocked grain. It is among the heaviest and hardest African woods, and one of the most resistant to decay and marine borers, making it a benchmark species for natural durability.

Common Uses: Thanks to its exceptional hardness and natural resistance to water and decay, it is typically chosen for projects requiring outstanding endurance and direct exposure to harsh environments, such as harbor and quay construction and heavy-duty industrial flooring, as well as railway sleepers where long-term durability is a priority.
